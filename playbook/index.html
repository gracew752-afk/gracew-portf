CH 01

The Core Broadcast Philosophy

Signal Chain

The Broadcast Mentality: treat your feed like a premium cable channel, not a random bulletin board. You are not just making posts — you are constructing a distinct, atmospheric universe an audience wants to tune into repeatedly.

CH 04

The 30-Minute Sprint Method

Block Breaker
⏱ RUN WHEN FRICTION OR CREATIVE BLOCK HITS

Lock One Topic

  • Example: "Why clean design can still feel completely soulless."

Generate 5 Angles

  • The Hot Take: "Minimalism is often just a cover for lack of ideas."
  • The Beginner Tip: "Use scale and contrast before removing elements."
  • The Common Mistake: "Confusing 'clean' with 'memorable.'"
  • The Before / After: "Redesigning a sterile landing page to have personality."
  • The Execution: "How I add character to minimal layouts."

Pick the Format

CarouselShort ReelText EssayStory SequenceMood Board

Write the Hook

  • "Pretty design is not the same as effective design."
  • "This is why your brand visuals look expensive but feel forgettable."
  • "A simple trick to give minimal design actual personality."

Deliver One Clear Takeaway

  • Keep the entire asset focused on a single actionable insight. No fluff.

Carousel Architect

Build a 10-slide carousel outline on: [TOPIC]. Slide 1 is the hook, slide 10 is the CTA ("save this"). For each slide give me: headline (max 8 words), supporting line (max 20 words), and a one-line visual direction that fits an editorial, monochrome-plus-one-accent layout.

Trend Scout

For Workflow Step 1 — visual research with live search.

I care about commercial direction, typography, and Y2K/digicam-adjacent aesthetics. Give me 5, ranked by relevance to a junior brand designer building a POV.

Gemini

For the Capture pipeline — analyzing saved inspo images.

Break down: (1) the visual vibe in 3 words, (2) lighting setup and how to recreate it on a Canon 90D, (3) color palette as hex codes, (4) typography or layout choices worth stealing, (5) one shoot or post idea this could inspire for a Y2K digicam-grunge brand.

For building small personal apps like The Call Sheet.

Any AI

For Friday breakdowns — "what I'd change" studies.

Any AI

CH 09

Spec Doctrine

Agency Radar From Nothing

The Audition Principle: a spec is not a fake ad — it's an audition tape for your judgment. The viewer is asking one question: can this person have an idea and execute it with taste? A gorgeous spec with no idea gets closed at second 8. An ugly spec with a killer idea gets forwarded around the office.

Idea First, Always

Every frame either serves the single idea or dies. Craft can't rescue a spot that isn't about anything.

Pick Brands Like a Strategist

Spec for brands with a famous, specific voice — so the audience can instantly judge whether you nailed it. Bonus: spec in the categories your target agencies actually service. It shows you did the homework on them.

Strategy Before Script

Write the single-minded proposition first — one sentence of brand truth. Being able to say why the spot works separates you from ten thousand kids with cameras. Speak both languages out loud: design and direction.

Constraints Are the Director

One location, 1–2 performers, natural light or one cheap source, 30–45 seconds max. Deadpan comedy is the low-budget director's best friend: stillness is free and timing costs nothing but skill.

Three, Not One

Agencies buy campaigns, not ads. One spec says "I got lucky once." Three spots in the same conceptual world says "I built a repeatable system — hand me a real brand." Two more executions of an existing universe beat three new ideas.

Distribution Is the Job

A spec on a hard drive is a diary entry. Agencies hire directors through production companies — a three-line email with a Vimeo link to a prod co rep is a legitimate, normal industry move.

Refuse to Wait for Permission

The spec that gets you noticed is never the one planned for six months. It's the one shot on a Tuesday. Make the next two before you talk yourself into a better plan.

Never Cheap Out — In This Order
  • PERFORMANCE — cast real deadpan talent, not friends doing bits.
  • SOUND — bad audio screams amateur louder than any visual flaw.
  • PACING — cut it 15% shorter than feels comfortable.
  • END CARD — bad type on a fake logo undoes everything. Your design background is the unfair advantage here.
Cheap Out Freely
  • LOCATIONS — one is enough. Restriction reads as style.
  • LIGHTING — natural light or one source, committed to fully.
  • GEAR — nobody hiring you asks what it was shot on.
  • RUNTIME — 30 seconds of tight beats 90 of loose, every time.

Screening Room

A clean Vimeo page — still the industry's default way to watch a reel.

Publications

Director's Notes · Booooooom · shots · LBBOnline — submit, don't wait to be found.

Competitions

D&AD New Blood · The One Club Young Ones — the two that agency recruiters genuinely monitor.

Production Companies

Prod cos sign emerging directors off spec reels. Short email, Vimeo link, specific ask.

The 90-Day Cadence
  1. NowAudit spot one against the craft list: sound, pacing, end card.
  2. Days 1–90Shoot spots two and three in the same conceptual universe.
  3. Day 90Cut a 60-second campaign reel from all three.
  4. Day 91+Submit to 2 competitions and 3 publications.
  5. OngoingFold the reel into outreach — "I made a three-spot campaign" beats "I'm looking for opportunities."

Director's Note

Nobody who hires directors cares about your gear, your budget, or your excuses. They care about one thing: can this person have an idea and execute it with taste. Spec work exists to answer that question. You've already made one. That instinct is the whole game.

Final Directive

"Don't try to be impressive in every post. Try to be clear, useful, and instantly recognizable. That is what builds a world."
